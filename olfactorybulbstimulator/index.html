OlfactoryBulbStimulator by Justus Verhagen

Rate model of the dorsal mouse OB as seen by wide-field fluorescence imaging. Input is the OSN axon-terminal signal (e.g. OMP-GCaMP); output is the mitral/tufted signal in the same glomeruli, with sister cells pooled. The model covers orthonasal stimulation only. Each glomerulus is a unitary relay with a Storace-lab input–output gain. Added to that: sniff-rate-dependent presynaptic adaptation (Verhagen et al. 2007), response dynamics that differ by glomerulus for both ORN input (Spors et al. 2006) and MTC output, and selective, non-random interglomerular inhibition (Economo et al. 2016). The model also has odorant mixtures and a constant background odorant. Odor maps are synthetic and chemotopically clustered.

Model & sources

Equations (dt = 10 ms, all glomeruli in both bulbs)
  • Stimulus: valves for odorants A and B (low-pass, τ = 50 ms) plus a constant background. The model warms up long enough for the background-adapted state to settle, and ΔF/F is taken relative to that adapted baseline, as in experiments. Sniffing is a sinusoidal inhalation over a fixed fraction of each cycle, with instantaneous rate f(t). Per-sniff peak flow scales as √(f/3 Hz). Faster sniffs are shallower but still deliver more odor per second.
  • Receptor activation, including mixtures: ug = cA/κgA + cB/κgB + cbg/κg,bg. Activation = 2·uⁿ/(1+uⁿ), i.e. competitive binding at one site with a receptor-specific Hill n (1–4). This gives mixture suppression and saturation within a glomerulus.
  • Sniff adaptive filter (Verhagen et al. 2007): a presynaptic resource Rg is depleted by the glomerulus's own lagged ORN output z (τ = 200 ms): dR/dt = (1−R)/τrec − Ug·z²·R, and the drive passed on is drive·R. Depletion lags each inhalation, so at about 1–2 Hz R recovers before the next sniff. At 5–8 Hz it cannot, so sustained or background odorants are attenuated while a newly arriving odor is still passed on (difference coding). The first sniff of a bout is transmitted in full (Spors et al. 2006).
  • ORN dynamics vary by glomerulus (Spors et al. 2006; Wachowiak & Cohen 2001): each glomerulus has its own latency, rise τ (15–400 ms), decay τ (40–1000 ms), and tonic adaptation (τ = 2.5 s). A slow decay τ means weak sniff modulation and a tonic response; a fast one means strongly sniff-locked, phasic input. Diversity is set by the "ORN dynamic diversity" slider (0 = identical).
  • SA cells: ORN̄ = ORN/(1 + wSA·μ), with μ the distance-weighted mean ORN activity (bulb-wide by default). Glomerular gain: drive = gMT·[ORN̄³/(κm³+ORN̄³) − β·ORN̄⁴·⁵/(κp⁴·⁵+ORN̄⁴·⁵)] (Subramanian et al.).
  • MTC output varies by glomerulus: tufted (τ = 30 ms) and mitral (τ = 80 ms, via an extra 50 ms ET stage) cells, with a tufted fraction specific to each glomerulus. Each glomerulus also has its own spike-frequency adaptation (τ = 1.5 s), which makes responses transient or sustained, and its own post-inhibitory rebound (τ = 0.4 s), which produces offset excitation and sag during suppression. Diversity is set by "MTC dynamic diversity".
  • Lateral inhibition following Economo et al. 2016: inhibition = sg·w·[(1−ψ)·Glocal + ψ·ksel·Gsel]. Glocal is recurrent GC inhibition, integrating Σ Kdist·(M+½T). Gsel is feed-forward interglomerular inhibition, integrating Σ Wsel·[excitatory glomerular drive]. Feed-forward wiring keeps strong selective inhibition stable. Both have a time constant specific to each glomerulus, which gives delayed suppression. Wsel is a sparse, fixed, non-random connectivity: glomeruli belong to latent "ensembles" and preferentially inhibit a partner ensemble, regardless of distance. Excited and suppressed glomeruli are therefore spatially intermingled, and only some glomeruli are strongly suppressible, because each has its own inhibition sensitivity sg.
  • Baseline: GC inhibition and MTC adaptation act on deviations from the spontaneous state, fixed 1.5 s into the warm-up before any odor. Every glomerulus therefore keeps spontaneous activity and can show suppression. The background odorant starts at 2 s, so its evoked inhibition and adaptation are included.
  • Indicators: asymmetric kinetics (fast rise τ, slow decay τ) feed saturating fluorescence; ΔF/F is taken against the 1 s before the first presentation.
  • Defaults: β = 0.3 and wSA = 2.5 (Subramanian et al. used 0.6 and 1; the published values can be set with the sliders). Not modeled: cholinergic and dopaminergic modulation, centrifugal feedback, retronasal stimulation.
Numbers and references
  • Count: about 2,850 glomeruli per mouse OB (Weng et al., Eur J Neurosci 2025). Wide-field imaging resolves about 100–200 per dorsal bulb: merging the maps of 40 natural odorants gave about 127 dorsal glomeruli (Vincis et al. 2012). The default here is 150 per bulb.

  • Spors, Wachowiak, Cohen & Friedrich 2006 J Neurosci 26:1247: glomerulus- and odorant-specific input dynamics.
  • Economo, Hansen & Wachowiak 2016 Neuron 91:397: selective, non-random interglomerular inhibition, intermingled excitation and suppression, and mixture suppression.
